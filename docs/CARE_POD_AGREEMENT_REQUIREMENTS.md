# Loop/90 Care Pod Professional Agreement Requirements

Status: Phase 1E business / product requirements. This is not legal advice and is not the final professional contract.

## 1. Purpose

Define the business, operational, product, compensation, safety, and data requirements that the eventual Care Pod professional agreement must cover.

A Pakistan-qualified lawyer should convert these requirements into the final enforceable agreement and review applicable healthcare, professional, tax, data, consumer, and employment / contractor issues before launch.

## 2. Parties and relationship

The final agreement should identify:

- MedLoop Technologies (Private) Limited / Loop/90 as applicable.
- The individual professional.
- Professional role: doctor, clinical nutritionist, or movement coach.
- Whether the relationship is independent contractor, service provider, employee, or another legally appropriate structure.

The agreement should not accidentally create promises inconsistent with the intended relationship.

## 3. Credential and eligibility requirements

Require, as applicable to role:

- Full legal name and identity verification.
- Professional registration / licence / certification details.
- Current status and expiry information where relevant.
- Relevant qualifications.
- Experience information.
- Bank / payout information.
- Tax information required for lawful settlement.
- Disclosure of conflicts or restrictions that materially affect the role.

Professional activation should be conditional on successful verification.

The product should eventually support a lifecycle such as:

APPLIED -> UNDER_REVIEW -> VERIFIED -> TRAINING_REQUIRED -> ACTIVE

with additional states such as REJECTED, SUSPENDED, EXPIRED_CREDENTIAL, and TERMINATED.

Exact naming should follow repository conventions.

## 4. Scope of role

The agreement must explicitly define role scope.

### Doctor

- Clinical oversight within the program.
- Glucose escalation review.
- Medication decisions.
- Clinical consultations.
- Required documentation.
- Safety / escalation obligations.

### Nutritionist

- Nutrition assessment and guidance within professional scope.
- Meal-plan work.
- Routed nutrition questions.
- Group sessions.
- Required documentation and handoff of medical issues.

### Movement coach

- Movement / activity guidance within professional scope.
- Agreed group sessions.
- Routed movement questions.
- Required documentation and handoff of medical issues.

No role should be described as responsible for matters outside its lawful professional scope.

## 5. AI and platform boundaries

The agreement should acknowledge that Loop/90 uses an AI-assisted layer for routine support, summarization, meal-photo analysis, and routing.

It should clearly state:

- AI is not the professional of record.
- AI does not make medication decisions.
- Professionals remain responsible for their own professional judgments and actions.
- Professionals must not blindly rely on AI output where professional review is required.
- Suspected AI errors / unsafe output should be reportable through an approved process.
- The platform may present AI-generated summaries to reduce administrative workload.

## 6. Care Pod assignment and cohort obligations

Define:

- How professionals are assigned to a Care Pod.
- Expected cohort size or approved range.
- 90-day program duration.
- Baseline workload / session expectations.
- Required Care Pod meetings.
- Availability expectations.
- Absence / leave process.
- Backup / substitution rules.
- Whether a professional may serve multiple cohorts simultaneously.
- Any maximum workload or capacity rule.

Avoid "unlimited availability" obligations.

## 7. Service levels

The agreement should incorporate or reference the approved role matrix.

Define:

- Urgent notification expectations.
- Amber / routed-work response expectations.
- Routine review cadence.
- Documentation timeframes.
- Group-session attendance / punctuality expectations.
- What happens if the professional cannot meet an SLA.

Exact patient-facing SLA promises should not be published until professional coverage supports them.

## 8. Compensation

The agreement must match the approved compensation specification.

It should separately define:

### Base cohort allocation

- Role-specific amount or formula.
- What baseline responsibilities it covers.
- Payment milestones.
- Conditions for payment.

### Additional activity compensation

- Which activities qualify.
- Fixed amount / formula.
- Verification requirement.
- Activities already included in base compensation.
- Prohibition on self-generating unnecessary activities for compensation.

### 1:1 appointments

- Which appointments are included in the program.
- Which are separately charged.
- Professional share / platform share.
- No-show / cancellation / refund treatment.
- When an appointment becomes payable.

### Quality / participation adjustment

- Measurable criteria.
- Green / Amber / Red definitions.
- Which component may be adjusted.
- Notice and review / dispute mechanism.
- No outcome-based incentives tied directly to remission, HbA1c reduction, medication reduction, or weight loss.

## 9. Payment administration

Define:

- Settlement schedule.
- Payment method.
- Required invoices / receipts if applicable.
- Tax withholding / deductions where legally required.
- Treatment of disputed items.
- Treatment of refunds and reversals.
- Minimum payout threshold if any.
- Payout statement / breakdown.

The platform should eventually provide an auditable line-item statement because the recruitment experience promises transparent payouts.

## 10. Clinical safety and escalation

The agreement should incorporate the approved escalation matrix and require professionals to:

- Act within role and competence.
- Follow Loop/90 escalation workflow.
- Escalate out-of-scope matters.
- Document material professional actions.
- Follow urgent-case procedures.
- Maintain appropriate professional standards.

For doctors, explicitly reserve medication decisions to authorized medical professionals.

The agreement should also state the platform's emergency-service limitations and how urgent patient guidance is handled.

## 11. Clinical autonomy and protocol

The recruitment proposition currently promises clinical autonomy within protocol.

The final agreement should therefore distinguish:

- Mandatory safety / operational rules.
- Program protocol / evidence framework.
- Areas where the professional exercises independent professional judgment.

The agreement should not force a professional to take an action they believe is unsafe or outside lawful practice. It should instead define escalation / exception handling.

## 12. Participant relationship and non-poaching expectations

The current recruitment proposition says Loop/90 is the lifestyle layer and does not seek to replace the participant's existing primary doctor relationship.

The agreement should define reasonable expectations regarding:

- Respecting existing patient-clinician relationships.
- Solicitation / poaching, if legally appropriate.
- Referrals to the professional's own practice.
- Continuity after the 90-day program.
- Participant choice and safety.

Any restrictive clause should be lawyer-reviewed for enforceability and fairness.

## 13. Data protection, confidentiality, and access

Define professional obligations for:

- Confidential participant information.
- Health-adjacent / sensitive data.
- Access only for assigned cohorts / participants.
- No sharing credentials.
- No exporting / copying data except where authorized.
- Secure devices and account practices.
- Reporting suspected breaches.
- Retention / deletion responsibilities.
- Platform monitoring / audit logs as appropriate.

The product already uses role-based access and RLS concepts; the contract should align with them.

## 14. Communication channels

Define approved communication channels.

Where possible, professional care should occur through the Loop/90 platform so that context, safety routing, and audit trails are preserved.

If WhatsApp, phone, video, or another channel is permitted, define:

- When it may be used.
- What must be documented back into the platform.
- Privacy expectations.
- Emergency limitations.

## 15. Professional conduct

Cover at minimum:

- Respectful communication.
- No harassment or discrimination.
- No misleading claims.
- No guarantee of remission or clinical outcomes.
- No use of cure language in conflict with Loop/90 policy.
- No unapproved promotion / sales to participants.
- No falsification of activities, attendance, or documentation.
- Compliance with applicable professional codes.

## 16. Insurance / indemnity positioning

The current professionals page explicitly avoids promising that Loop/90 provides professional indemnity cover.

The agreement should clearly state the actual insurance / indemnity position after legal review.

Do not claim a medico-legal shield or insurance protection that does not exist.

## 17. Intellectual property and content

Define ownership / permitted use for:

- Program protocols.
- Training materials.
- Professional-created materials.
- Recorded group sessions if recording is allowed.
- Platform content.
- Participant-specific plans.

Obtain explicit consent before using a professional's name, image, biography, credentials, testimonials, or recorded content in marketing.

## 18. Training / Care Pod Academy

Activation may require completion of a focused Care Pod Academy / protocol onboarding covering:

- Loop/90 program model.
- Role boundaries.
- Platform usage.
- AI boundaries.
- Escalation protocol.
- Documentation.
- Communication standards.
- Compensation rules.
- Data / privacy expectations.

The product should eventually record training completion and version of protocol accepted.

## 19. Quality review and remediation

Define a fair process for:

- Quality measurement.
- Notice of Amber / Red concerns.
- Remediation / coaching.
- Repeated missed SLAs.
- Participant complaints.
- Safety concerns.
- Temporary suspension.
- Care Pod replacement.
- Appeal / dispute where appropriate.

Do not use hidden quality criteria.

## 20. Withdrawal, suspension, and termination

Cover:

- Professional voluntary withdrawal.
- Required notice.
- Immediate suspension for serious safety / credential issues.
- Termination for material breach.
- Handover of active participants.
- Treatment of earned but unpaid compensation.
- Treatment of incomplete activities.
- Data access after termination.

Patient continuity and safety should take priority during handover.

## 21. No guaranteed earnings

Recruitment materials and the agreement should avoid guaranteed income claims.

Any examples should be clearly illustrative and dependent on actual cohort assignment, completed eligible work, appointment volume, payment collection, and approved compensation rules.

## 22. Complaints and disputes

Define:

- Operational support channel.
- Compensation dispute process.
- Clinical / safety complaint escalation.
- Participant complaint handling.
- Governing law / jurisdiction or dispute mechanism after legal review.

## 23. Product requirements derived from the agreement

The eventual product will likely need to support:

- Professional application status.
- Credential records and expiry.
- Verification decision and audit.
- Training completion.
- Agreement version and acceptance timestamp.
- Care Pod assignment.
- Workload / SLA events.
- Professional activities.
- Quality review.
- Payout calculation and statement.
- Suspension / termination state.

These should be implemented only after the business rules and final legal agreement are approved.

## 24. Legal-review checklist before launch

Ask Pakistan-qualified counsel to review at minimum:

1. Correct legal relationship with each professional role.
2. Healthcare / telehealth implications of the model.
3. Professional licensing and scope requirements.
4. Data protection / sensitive health-data obligations.
5. Tax / withholding treatment of payouts.
6. Consumer implications of separately paid appointments.
7. Liability / indemnity language.
8. Non-solicitation / restrictive clauses.
9. Recording / content consent.
10. Termination and dispute provisions.

## 25. Business decisions required before legal drafting

1. Final Cohort #1 role payouts.
2. Exact baseline workload allowances.
3. Activity compensation catalogue and rates.
4. Appointment pricing / split.
5. Payment milestones.
6. Amber / Red compensation treatment.
7. Refund / cancellation treatment.
8. Professional coverage / backup model.
9. Care Pod Academy requirements.
10. Actual insurance / indemnity position.
