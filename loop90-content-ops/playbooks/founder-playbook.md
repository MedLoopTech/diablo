# Playbook — Founder

You own the decisions no skill can make for you.

---

## What only you decide

| Decision | Why it's yours |
|----------|----------------|
| Adding or changing a controlled term | Legal and medical exposure |
| Publishing something a gate blocked | Only you can accept that risk |
| Whether a new pillar belongs | Strategy, not content |
| Pricing, currency, referral terms | You own the numbers; content verifies them |
| Whether a persona exists | Market definition |
| Promoting a piece to `examples/gold/` | Sets the standard for everything after |

---

## Getting content made

```
Ask the CEO skill. Don't write the brief yourself.
```

Give it the request in plain language. If it's vague, the strategist will come back with questions — that's the system working, not friction.

**Don't** hand a finished draft to `publishing`. It'll be sent back for a brief and a memo, and you'll have wasted the writing.

---

## When a gate blocks you

The gate will tell you exactly what's wrong and usually how to fix it. Most blocks are ten-minute fixes — a missing definition, a rephrased guarantee.

If you genuinely want to publish something a gate blocked, that's your call to make **explicitly**. Say so directly. No skill will do it silently on your behalf, and none should.

Before overriding, ask: would I be comfortable defending this sentence to a doctor, a regulator, or a patient who acted on it?

---

## The three facts most likely to go wrong

Content can't verify these — you can:

1. **Pricing** — changes, and `PITCHES.md` is a document not a live config
2. **Currency** — admin-configurable; content that hardcodes PKR breaks when it's changed
3. **Referral payouts** — per-code and variable; there is no single universal figure

When `publishing` holds a piece asking you to confirm one of these, that's the system catching a real risk.

---

## Quarterly, 30 minutes

Run the sync audit in `sops/terminology-governance-sop.md`:

1. Read the live `BANNED_CLAIMS` array in the app
2. Diff against the content repo
3. Fix drift in whichever direction is wrong
4. Record it in the changelog

This is the only recurring maintenance the system needs.

---

## Reading the quality signal

| Score | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| ≥ 95 | Gold candidate — worth annotating as a standard |
| 85–94 | Good, ships |
| 70–84 | Something's off — usually the brief, not the writing |
| Repeated blocks on one piece | Scope disagreement upstream; re-brief it |

If most pieces score 86–88, the rubric is working. If everything scores 95+, someone is scoring generously.

---

## What to resist

- Asking for content "quickly, just this once" — the gates are the product's credibility
- Approving a claim because it would convert well
- Letting a keyword drive a medical claim
- Naming competitors negatively, however tempting
