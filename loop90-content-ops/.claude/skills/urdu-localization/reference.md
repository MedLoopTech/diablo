## Foods — always keep the Urdu name

Roti, chapati, paratha, chawal, biryani, pulao, daal, sabzi, kachumber, raita, lassi, chai, nihari, haleem, mithai, jalebi, samosa, suhoor, iftar.

Never translate these into English descriptions in Urdu content ("lentil curry" for daal is absurd in an Urdu sentence).

## Phrases that carry the brand voice

| Intent | Roman Urdu |
|--------|------------|
| No guilt for a slip | *"Koi baat nahi. Aaj se dobara shuru."* |
| Start small | *"Thoda sa shuru karein."* |
| Consistency | *"Roz thoda, hamesha behtar."* |
| Encouragement | *"Aap theek ja rahe hain."* |
| Route to doctor | *"Yeh aapke doctor se poochne wali baat hai."* |
| Urgency without panic | *"Yeh urgent hai — abhi apne doctor se rabta karein."* |

## Danger patterns

| Pattern | Why it's unsafe | Instead |
|---------|-----------------|---------|
| *"Sugar khatam ho jayegi"* | Cure claim | *"Sugar control mein aa sakti hai"* |
| *"Ilaj"* for remission | Literally "cure" | Loanword "remission" + explanation |
| *"Bilkul theek ho jayenge"* | Guaranteed outcome | *"Bohot logon ki halat behtar hoti hai"* |
| *"Dawai chhor dein"* | Medication advice | *"Dawai ke baare mein doctor faisla karenge"* |
| Dropping *"ho sakta hai"* | Removes the hedge | Keep every hedge explicit |

The last one is subtle and common: casual Urdu naturally drops modal hedging. *"Isse sugar kam hoti hai"* asserts; *"Isse sugar kam ho sakti hai"* hedges. The English source's hedge must survive.

## Numbers and units

- Keep Western numerals (250, 70, 180) — universally read
- Keep mg/dL as-is
- Never spell out a threshold in words; ambiguity here is dangerous

## Code-switching density

Aim for what a Karachi household actually speaks: English for medical and technical nouns, Urdu for everything connective and emotional.

Too much English: *"Aapki fasting glucose reading elevated hai, please monitor karein."*
Too much Urdu: *"Aapki nihar munh khoon ki shakkar ki padhat buland hai."*
Right: *"Aapki subah wali sugar reading thori zyada hai. Kal phir note karein."*

## Channels that stay English

Doctor, pharma, and coach-recruitment content is professional register — English only. Roman Urdu in a LinkedIn post to an endocrinologist reads as unprofessional, not warm.
