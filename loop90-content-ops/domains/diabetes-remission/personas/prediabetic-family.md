# Persona 2 — Prediabetes / At-Risk Family Member

Prevention audience. Often the son, daughter, or spouse of a Persona 1 patient — sometimes the person who found Loop/90 first and brought it home.

## Profile

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Age | 28–45 |
| Location | Urban Pakistan; some overseas (UAE, UK) with family here |
| Status | Prediabetic (HbA1c 5.7–6.4%), or normal labs with strong family history |
| Medication | None |
| Trigger | A parent's diagnosis, a scary annual-checkup number, or a wedding photo |
| Device | Android or iPhone, active on Instagram and WhatsApp |
| English | Comfortable; consumes English health content |

## What they actually want

1. To not end up like their parent
2. A number they can watch and move
3. Proof this is real science, not another wellness fad
4. Something they can do without announcing it to the whole family
5. To help their parent without nagging them

## Emotional state

Anxious rather than defeated — the opposite of Persona 1. Motivated by *avoiding* a future, not escaping a present. Researches before committing. Skeptical of anything that smells like an MLM or influencer supplement pitch. Will read a citation.

## Objections to pre-empt

| Objection | Response angle |
|-----------|----------------|
| "I don't have diabetes, this isn't for me." | Prediabetes is the window where reversal is easiest. Name the window. |
| "Is this evidence-based or just marketing?" | Cite DiRECT, Roy Taylor. Show evidence level. Never name-drop for hype. |
| "My parent won't listen to me." | Give them a shareable, non-preachy asset — the guide, not a lecture. |
| "I'm already busy and healthy-ish." | Small, specific, low-friction: post-meal walk, plate ratio, one swap. |
| "Isn't this genetic anyway?" | Family history raises risk; it doesn't decide the outcome. Explain honestly. |

## Voice cues

- Grade 8–10, slightly more technical than Persona 1
- Evidence-forward — this persona *wants* the citation
- Less hand-holding, more "here's the mechanism, here's the lever"
- Still desi-first on food, but can handle GI/GL tables
- No fear-mongering — anxiety is already the baseline

## Channels (priority)

Blog / SEO ★★★★★ · Instagram ★★★★ · Lead magnet ★★★★★ · Email ★★★★ · Short video ★★★★

## Content that works

- "Your HbA1c is 6.0. Here's what that actually means."
- Family-history explainers with real risk numbers
- The bilingual plate guide as a shareable asset for parents
- Myth-busting with citations
- "How to talk to your father about his diabetes without a fight"

## Content that fails

- Patient-style motivational copy (they aren't struggling yet)
- Anything that treats prediabetes as inevitable progression
- Oversimplified advice with no mechanism

## Conversion path

Primary: lead magnet (Desi Diabetes Plate Guide) → email nurture → cohort enrollment or a referral for their parent. This persona is also the highest-value **referrer** — content should make sharing easy and non-awkward.

## Safety notes

- Never diagnose from content. "Talk to your doctor about a HbA1c test" is the CTA, not a self-assessment quiz that returns a verdict.
- Same disclaimer block as all medical content.
