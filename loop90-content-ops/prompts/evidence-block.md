# Prompt Block — Evidence

For medical content. Full standard in `research/evidence-levels.md`.

---

## Levels and the language each permits

| Level | Source type | You may write |
|-------|-------------|---------------|
| **A** | Meta-analysis, systematic review, good RCT | "research shows", "a randomised trial found" |
| **B** | Cohort, case-control, small RCT | "studies suggest", "associated with" — never "proven", never "causes" |
| **C** | Case series, mechanism, animal, expert opinion | "early evidence suggests", "the proposed mechanism is" — must be labelled preliminary in the body |
| **Consensus** | ADA, WHO, IDF, EASD, NICE, PES | "guidelines recommend", "standard practice is" — name the body and year |

## The numbers rule

Every figure in the draft must appear in the research memo's numbers table, with the exact wording approved there. No rounding, no restating, no recalling. If it isn't in the table, it doesn't go in the draft.

## Citation format

Inline:

> Losing 15kg or more put 86% of participants into remission at one year (Lean et al., *The Lancet*, 2018).

Reference list entry:

```
1. Lean MEJ, et al. Primary care-led weight management for remission of type 2
   diabetes (DiRECT). The Lancet. 2018;391(10120):541-551.
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29221645/  [Level A]
```

## Source tiers

| Tier | Use |
|------|-----|
| PubMed journals, Cochrane, guideline bodies | Anything |
| University / hospital publications, WHO / IDF | Anything |
| Clinician-educators (Taylor, Fung, Attia, Bikman, Westman) | Framing and mechanism only — never the sole source for a number |
| News, blogs, brand content, AI summaries, Wikipedia | Never |

## The remission definition (verbatim)

> Remission means an HbA1c below 6.5% sustained for at least three months without glucose-lowering medication.

Never restate loosely. Never imply permanent or guaranteed.

## Counter-evidence is mandatory

Every medical piece acknowledges what the research does **not** show, where it's contested, and who it doesn't apply to. An article presenting only supporting evidence fails rubric A1.

## Hedging

| Instead of | Write |
|------------|-------|
| "Studies prove" (Level B) | "Studies suggest" |
| "X causes Y" (observational) | "X is associated with Y" |
| "You will reach remission" | "Many people reach remission; whether you do depends on factors your doctor can assess" |
| "Everyone benefits" | "Most participants in the trial improved" |
