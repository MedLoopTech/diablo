## The copy

> **The 1/2 – 1/4 – 1/4 Desi Plate Rule**
>
> Transform any standard *ghar ka khana* into a diabetes-reversing meal by arranging your plate in these exact proportions:
>
> **1/2 Fiber & Veggies** — Kachumber Salad (Kheera, Tomato, Onion) · Bhindi, Karela, Tinda, Palak Sabzi · Baingan Bharta or Lauki
>
> **1/4 Protein & Clean Fats** — Chicken / Beef / Mutton Karahi (moderated oil) · Chana Dal, Daal Mash, Rajma · Eggs, Fish, or Paneer Tikka
>
> **1/4 Controlled Carbs** — Pick ONE per meal. Never mix double starches!
> 1 Medium Whole-Wheat Tawa Roti **OR** ½ Cup Cooked Basmati Rice
>
> *Rule: Never combine Roti + Rice in 1 meal*

## Annotations

**[Named foods, not categories]** *Kachumber, bhindi, karela, tinda, palak, baingan bharta, lauki, daal mash, rajma.* Nine specific dishes, zero generic categories.

This is what "desi kitchen first" means operationally. Compare the failure mode: *"Fill half your plate with non-starchy vegetables."* Technically identical, practically useless — it makes the reader do the translation work, and most won't.

**[*Ghar ka khana*]** Code-switching doing real work. "Transform any standard home-cooked meal" is correct English and lands with a fraction of the force. The Urdu phrase says *we mean your actual kitchen, not a diet version of it.*

**[The one-carb rule]** *"Never combine Roti + Rice in 1 meal"*

Binary, memorable, checkable at the table without measuring anything. This is what rubric E1 is asking for — one action, no decisions required.

Contrast the version that fails: *"Moderate your total carbohydrate load per meal."* Same physiology, unusable.

**[Geometry over arithmetic]** Portions as plate fractions, not grams or calories. No scale, no app, no counting. The single highest-leverage format choice in the whole guide.

**[The named exception]** *"If eating Biryani, double the salad!"* — appears later in the guide. Biryani isn't forbidden; it's accounted for. Nothing loses a reader faster than banning the food their family cooks on Sundays.

## Reusable pattern

```
1. Name the geometry (fractions, not grams)
2. Fill each fraction with 3+ named local dishes
3. One binary rule that needs no measuring
4. Name the hard case and account for it, don't ban it
```

---

## The lesson

Specificity is a safety feature, not just a style preference. "Eat more vegetables" gets interpreted; "kachumber, bhindi, karela" gets cooked. The more precisely you name the food, the less room there is for the advice to drift into something that doesn't work.
