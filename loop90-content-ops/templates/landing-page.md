# Template — Landing Page

★★★★★ The only channel where "reverse" and "guaranteed" are conditionally allowed — and only under strict terms.

## Constraints

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Length | 800–1500 words |
| Reading level | Grade 8–10 |
| CTAs | One primary, repeated 3–4× |
| Disclaimer | Footer **and** adjacent to health claims |
| Mobile | Designed mobile-first; check layout before ship |

## Structure

```
1. Hero          — headline + subhead + primary CTA
2. Problem       — their situation in their words
3. Solution      — what Loop/90 actually is
4. How it works  — 3 steps or 3 phases
5. Proof         — evidence + care pod + (careful) testimonial
6. Objections    — 4–6 from the persona file
7. Pricing       — accurate, verified, not recalled
8. FAQ           — 5–8 real questions
9. Final CTA
10. Disclaimer + legal
```

## Required elements

- [ ] Headline states the outcome without promising it
- [ ] Care pod described accurately: one doctor, one nutritionist, one movement coach
- [ ] Cohort size 30–50, program 90 days
- [ ] Pricing verified against current source
- [ ] Remission defined where the word first appears
- [ ] Disclaimer in footer + near every health claim
- [ ] Objections drawn from the loaded persona file

## The "reverse" / "guaranteed" exception

Permitted **only** on B2C landing pages, and only when all of these hold:

1. "Remission" is defined on the same page (HbA1c < 6.5% for 3+ months without glucose-lowering medication)
2. "Guaranteed" refers to the **money-back term**, never a health outcome
3. Legal terms appear in the footer on the same page
4. The disclaimer block is present

If any condition fails, use "remission" and drop "guaranteed" entirely. Doctor-facing and pharma landing pages get **no** exception.

## Headline patterns that work

- "90 days. One care pod. A real shot at remission."
- "Your doctor sees you twice a year. We're there the other 363 days."
- "Built for roti — not quinoa."

## Headline patterns to avoid

- Anything with a number of kilos or HbA1c points as a promise
- "Cure your diabetes in 90 days"
- Fear openers ("Diabetes is killing you slowly")

## Proof section rules

- Evidence: cite DiRECT properly, with the level
- Testimonials: allowed, but any specific number requires "individual results vary" adjacent
- Never a before/after image implying a typical result
- Care pod credentials are stronger proof than testimonials — lead with them

## Pricing block

State: what's included, cohort model, currency as configured. Never invent or recall a number — verify it. If a per-code or per-partner amount is involved, it varies; don't state a single figure as universal.

## Final disclaimer (verbatim)

> Loop/90 supports your care team — it does not replace medical advice. Medication changes only ever come from your doctor. This content is for education, not diagnosis or treatment. If you feel unwell or your glucose is very high or very low, contact your doctor or emergency services.
