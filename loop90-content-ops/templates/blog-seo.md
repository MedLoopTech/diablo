# Template — Blog / SEO Article

★★★★ The long-form medical content channel. Full rubric applies at 85/100.

## Constraints

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Length | 1200–2000 words |
| Reading level | Grade 8–10 (with glossary for technical terms) |
| References | 2–5, PubMed or major guideline |
| Evidence level | Stated in frontmatter |
| Disclaimer | Full block at end |

## Structure

```
H1 — contains primary keyword, reads naturally

Intro (100–150 words)
  Hook in the first two sentences
  What this article answers
  Who it's for

H2 — The core concept
  Mechanism in 2–3 sentences, not a lecture
  Evidence with citation

H2 — What this means in a Pakistani kitchen
  Concrete: roti, chawal, daal, biryani, chai
  Portion and pairing, not elimination

H2 — What the research actually shows
  Findings with levels
  Limits and who it doesn't apply to

H2 — Try this today
  One action, specific, realistic

H2 — When to talk to your doctor
  Clinical note, thresholds if relevant

FAQ (3–5 questions, real search queries)

References
Disclaimer block
```

## Required elements

- [ ] Primary keyword in H1, first paragraph, and one H2
- [ ] Meta description under 160 chars
- [ ] Mechanism explained in 2–3 sentences max
- [ ] Desi food section — not optional
- [ ] Counter-evidence / limits section
- [ ] "Try this today" — one action
- [ ] Clinical note: when to involve the doctor
- [ ] 2–5 references with levels
- [ ] Full disclaimer block
- [ ] Internal links to 2–3 related pieces
- [ ] Alt text on every image

## SEO rules

- Write for the reader first; keyword placement second
- Never keyword-stuff — `editing` will flag it under D1
- One primary keyword per article; don't compete with your own pages
- H2s should read as questions a person would actually type
- Slug: short, readable, keyword-bearing (`/roti-blood-sugar-guide`)

## Evidence discipline

Every number comes from the research memo's numbers table with its exact approved wording. If it isn't in the memo, it doesn't go in the article.

## Never

- Unsourced statistics
- "Studies prove" for Level B evidence
- Name-dropping an author in place of a citation
- A conclusion the evidence section doesn't support
- Western meal examples as the default
