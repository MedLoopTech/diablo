# Template — LinkedIn

★★★★ Professional channel. Personas: doctor, nutritionist-coach, pharma-corporate. **Never** patient-facing.

## Constraints

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Length | 150–400 words (post); 600–1200 (article) |
| Reading level | Professional |
| Tone | Peer-to-peer, no patient warmth |
| Hook | First 2 lines — everything after is behind "…see more" |
| CTAs | One, low-friction |
| Disclaimer | "Clinical decisions remain yours" on doctor-facing content |

## Structure

```
Hook (2 lines max — this is all most people see)

Context: the problem in their language

Substance: 3–5 short paragraphs or a tight list

What we built / what we found

One question or one low-friction CTA
```

## Variants

### Doctor recruitment

Lead with the monitoring gap, never with technology.

> Your diabetic patients are seen every 3–6 months.
> A lot goes wrong in three months.
>
> The ones who are deteriorating rarely call. They wait until it's bad enough for a visit. And when they arrive, there's no glucose history — just what they remember.
>
> Loop/90 gives you the readings between visits, flagged by severity, with a full medication audit trail. Clinical decisions remain yours; the AI never touches medication.
>
> 30-day trial, we onboard your first 10 patients on the call.

### Pod staff recruitment

Concrete on income, hours, scope.

### Thought leadership

Evidence-forward, no product pitch. Build authority; mention Loop/90 once at most, or not at all.

### Pharma / partnership

Adherence economics. Executive register. Data-forward.

## Required elements

- [ ] Hook works in 2 lines
- [ ] Professional register throughout — no "Salaam", no motivational copy
- [ ] Claims sourced where they're numbers
- [ ] "Clinical decisions remain yours" on doctor content
- [ ] What the AI does **not** do, stated explicitly
- [ ] One CTA

## Formatting

- Short paragraphs, one idea each
- Line breaks over walls of text
- No emoji in doctor or pharma content; sparing in coach content
- No hashtag spam — 3 max, relevant

## Never

- Patient testimonials as the primary proof to a clinical audience
- "Reverse" or "guaranteed" — banned entirely on this channel
- Implying the platform diagnoses, prescribes, or replaces judgment
- Overselling AI capability
- Consumer wellness aesthetics
