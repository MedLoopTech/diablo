# SOP — Gold Example Promotion

`examples/gold/` sets the standard everything else is measured against. What goes in matters more than how much.

---

## Eligibility

| Requirement | Threshold |
|-------------|-----------|
| Rubric score | ≥ 95/100 |
| Safety verdict | `cleared`, no blocks in its history |
| Status | Published |
| Nominated by | `publishing` (flag only — never self-promotes) |

Score alone isn't sufficient. A 96 that's competent-but-unremarkable teaches nothing.

---

## What makes a piece gold

It must be **exemplary at something specific** — a reason someone would study it:

| Exemplary at | Looks like |
|--------------|------------|
| Desi specificity | Every example is a real dish, real preparation, real household context |
| Actionability | The action is one ingredient or one 10-minute habit, not a behaviour change |
| Honest hedging | Caveats that genuinely qualify without undermining the takeaway |
| Voice | Warmth with no shame; hope with no promise |
| Mechanism economy | Complex physiology in three sentences that a grade-8 reader follows |
| Safe routing | Medication or fasting handled warmly and correctly |

A piece that's merely *good everywhere* is not gold. Gold is *outstanding somewhere*.

---

## Promotion procedure

1. **`publishing` flags** the candidate (does not promote)
2. **Founder or clinician reviews** — is this the standard we want copied?
3. **Annotate** — this is the actual work (below)
4. **File** in `examples/gold/` with its annotations
5. **Index** in `examples/gold/README.md`, naming what it exemplifies

---

## Annotation

An unannotated example teaches by imitation, which copies the surface. Annotations name the decision.

Annotate inline:

```markdown
> You've probably noticed it yourself. Biryani sends your number up.
> Daal chawal, somehow, doesn't do it the same way.

**[Opening]** Starts with the reader's own observation, not a definition
or a statistic. They already noticed this — we're explaining, not
informing. Compare the weak alternative: "Glycaemic load is an important
concept in diabetes management."
```

Cover at minimum:

- Why the opening works
- Where the mechanism stops and the application starts
- How a caveat was placed without deflating the piece
- Why this specific action was chosen over a general one
- Any place the writer wanted more and the memo said no

---

## Cap the collection

| Category | Max |
|----------|-----|
| Per channel | 2–3 |
| Per persona | 2–3 |
| Total | ~15 |

When a better example arrives, **replace** rather than accumulate. Twenty examples means nobody reads any of them.

Retire an example when: it's superseded by a better one, its evidence has decayed, or the brand voice has moved on.

---

## Also keep before/after pairs

`examples/before-after/` — a weak draft next to its edited version, with the rubric deltas. These teach *editing* in a way finished pieces can't.

Source them from real `editing` returns, anonymised if needed.

---

## The test

Hand a new writer the gold folder and nothing else. Could they produce something on-voice, on-safety, and on-standard?

If not, the examples aren't annotated well enough — or they're the wrong examples.
